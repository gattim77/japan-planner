# Festival research audit

Reviewed October 1, 2026. The catalogue now contains 147 festival records across all 47 prefectures, up from nine records. This is broad national coverage, not a claim to enumerate every neighbourhood festival in Japan.

## Research and evidence

Reviewed 132 individual English festival pages in the official All Japan & Tokyo tourism directory, a joint prefectural and Tokyo tourism resource. Added major celebrations from official local tourism, Kyoto tourism, Sapporo tourism and JNTO sources. Duplicate listings were reconciled with existing IDs; distinct processions and separately dated seasonal editions retain separate records. The source index below links every retained record. Descriptions are brief editorial summaries, not copied articles.

- **Announced:** the linked source explicitly names the occurrence year and dates. This is evidence of an announcement, not a guarantee against later cancellation.
- **Expected annual dates:** derived from a fixed annual date or an unambiguous weekday rule. These require a future programme check.
- **Season · dates pending:** a possible discovery period for dates that vary, broad seasonal windows, or conflicting/old schedules. The period does not mean an event runs every day. These records cannot satisfy hard attendance constraints or contribute a dated attendance score.

For the reference window October 1, 2026–October 1, 2027, there are 150 matching occurrences: 21 announced, 71 expected and 58 seasonal. More occurrences than records can result from overlapping annual endpoints and cross-year seasons.

## Review decisions

Sapporo Snow Festival has explicit February 4–11, 2027 evidence. Kiritampo and Soma listings contain conflicting year/weekday information and remain seasonal. Kanda's main procession follows odd-year recurrence; Sanno's larger procession is even-year, with annual shrine rituals separately explained. Hamasaki uses the weekend after the third Monday in July, rather than the fourth Saturday. Hachinohe crosses July/August. Noto Kiriko is labelled as a regional series and carries a recovery/programme caveat.

Map coordinates generally use the official listing's map pin. Supplementary city-centre pins are labelled approximate; these are not procession-route geometry. New destinations are available for discovery, while the transport optimizer remains limited to its ten curated route cities. Uncertain and unsupported festivals can be saved as interests, but cannot be added as hard itinerary constraints. No claim of live transport coverage is made.
